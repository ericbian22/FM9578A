1 · Symbols & cash-flow timing

$S_0,S_t,S_T$: underlying now, at $t$, at expiry; $K$: strike; $T$: years; $r$: continuous domestic risk-free rate. $c,p$: European call/put; $C,P$: American.

PV of strike $K$ at $T$: $Ke^{-rT}$; future value of $K$: $Ke^{rT}$. $D$: PV of known dividends; $q$: continuous dividend yield; $r_f$: foreign rate.

Payoff = expiry cash flow. Profit = payoff minus cost (same time basis). Premium at $0$: subtract premium for unfinanced convention, or premium $e^{rT}$ at expiry if financed.

2 · Contracts & four positions

Forward: obligation; long $S_T-K$, short $K-S_T$. Futures: standardized, daily mark-to-market. Option: buyer’s right, writer’s obligation; premium upfront.

European: exercise at $T$ only. American: may exercise earlier. Hedger reduces exposure; speculator takes risk; arbitrage buys cheap/sells expensive equivalent cash flows.

Long positionPayoffBE*
Call$\max(S_T-K,0)$$K+c_0$
Put$\max(K-S_T,0)$$K-p_0$

Short = negative long payoff/profit. *BE ignores financing. Long option max loss = premium; call upside unbounded; put payoff at most $K$.

ITM call $S>K$; ITM put $S<K$; ATM $S=K$. ITM ≠ profitable. Intrinsic = exercise payoff; time value = price − intrinsic.

Q · Buy a call: payoff/profit? $K=50$, premium $4$; ignore interest. At $S_T=62$: payoff $62-50=12$, profit $12-4=8$ per share. At $48$: do not exercise; payoff $0$, loss = premium $4$.

3 · Trading & value drivers

Multiply per-unit amounts by contract multiplier (lecture stock convention: 100). Class: call/put on underlying; series: same strike/expiry. Close long by selling same series; close short by buying it.

Q · Bid $4$, ask $4.50$: trading cost? Buy at ask, sell at bid: spread $4.50-4=.50$; midpoint $(4+4.50)/2=4.25$. Immediate 100-share round trip loses $100(.50)=50$ before fees.

Mark-to-market: observed quote; mark-to-model: model inputs. Margin is collateral. Initial deposit; maintenance threshold; margin call restores required balance. Futures/writers can face obligations; fully paid option buyer loses at most premium.

4 · Bounds: no dividends

Frictionless trade, shorting and borrowing/lending at $r$; same underlying/strike/expiry for comparisons.

$\max(S_0-Ke^{-rT},0)\le c\le S_0$
$\max(Ke^{-rT}-S_0,0)\le p\le Ke^{-rT}$

American: $\max(S_0-K,0)\le C\le S_0$; $\max(K-S_0,0)\le P\le K$ for $r\ge0$.

Proof pattern: call + bond paying $K$ dominates stock; put + stock dominates bond. Buy underpriced dominating portfolio, sell dominated one.

5 · Parity, dividends & exercise

$c+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0$ (European, no dividends). Both sides pay $\max(S_T,K)$.

$c=p+S_0-Ke^{-rT}$
$p=c+Ke^{-rT}-S_0$
$S_0=c-p+Ke^{-rT}$.

Arbitrage: sell expensive side, buy cheap side; initial receipt, terminal cancellation. Negative bond term = borrow.

Known dividends: $D=$ their PV.
$c+D+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0$
$c\ge\max(S_0-D-Ke^{-rT},0)$
$p\ge\max(D+Ke^{-rT}-S_0,0)$.

American bounds ($r\ge0$):
$S_0-K\le C-P\le S_0-Ke^{-rT}$
with dividends, lower bound $S_0-D-K$.

No-dividend call, $r\ge0$: $C=c$; do not exercise early (lose optionality, pay strike sooner). Put may exercise deep ITM to receive $K$ earlier. Dividends may favor call exercise just before ex-dividend. Always compare continuation.

At a cash dividend: $S_{\rm after}=S_{\rm before}-\text{cash dividend}$. Cash amount ≠ PV ≠ continuous yield. A yield CRR tree is not an exact cash-dividend tree.

6 · Strategy quick reference

Same expiry unless noted. $K_1<K_2<K_3$; net debit = cost paid. Profit below ignores financing. Add signed legs, split at strikes, subtract net cost.

Spreads: max loss = net cost; max gain = strike width − cost; bull BE = low strike + cost; bear BE = high strike − cost.

European box: bull call + bear put. Constant payoff $K_2-K_1$, value $(K_2-K_1)e^{-rT}$. American early exercise complicates replication.

Butterfly: $c(K_1)-2c(K_2)+c(K_3)$, equal spacing. Peak payoff $K_2-K_1$ at $K_2$; zero outside wings. Low-movement view.

Q · Butterfly cost/profit/BEs? Calls at strikes $90,100,110$ cost $14.2,8.1,4.3$. Buy 1 low + 1 high, sell 2 middle: cost $14.2-2(8.1)+4.3=2.3$. Peak payoff $10$ at $S_T=100$ → max profit $10-2.3=7.7$. BEs $90+2.3=92.3$, $110-2.3=107.7$.

Straddle: $c(K)+p(K)$, payoff $|S_T-K|$; BEs $K\pm(c+p)$.

Strangle: $p(K_1)+c(K_2)$; payoff $\max(K_1-S_T,0)+\max(S_T-K_2,0)$; BEs: low strike − cost; high strike + cost. Lower cost, larger move needed.

Strip: call + 2 puts (down bias): $\max(S_T-K,0)+2\max(K-S_T,0)$. Strap: 2 calls + put (up bias): $2\max(S_T-K,0)+\max(K-S_T,0)$.

Calendar: short near, long far, same strike. Far leg retains time value at near expiry. Diagonal: different strikes and expiries.

Principal protection: budget $100$ → bond $100e^{-rT}$ + call budget $100(1-e^{-rT})$, assuming nonnegative budget and risk-free bond.

7 · One-step replication · no yield

$S_u=S_0u$, $S_d=S_0d$; $f_u,f_d$ option values; $\Delta$ shares per option unit.

$\Delta=(f_u-f_d)/(S_u-S_d)$
$\Pi_u=\Delta S_u-f_u=\Pi_d=\Delta S_d-f_d$
$f=\Delta S_0-(\Delta S_u-f_u)e^{-r\Delta t}$.

Long $\Delta$ shares − one option is riskless. To hedge a short of 1,000 option units, hold $1000\Delta$ shares (negative means short shares).

Q · Price/hedge a 3-month call? $S_0=20$, up $22$, down $18$, $K=21,r=.04$. Payoffs $1,0$ → $\Delta=(1-0)/(22-18)=.25$. Buy .25 shares, short one call: both states pay $22(.25)-1=18(.25)=4.5$. Thus $5-f=4.5e^{-.01}$ → $f=.5448$.

8 · Risk-neutral pricing

$p=(a-d)/(u-d)$; require $d<a<u$ so $0<p<1$.

$f=e^{-r\Delta t}[pf_u+(1-p)f_d]$

UnderlyingGrowth $a$
No-dividend stock$e^{r\Delta t}$
Yield stock / index$e^{(r-q)\Delta t}$
FX (domestic/foreign)$e^{(r-r_f)\Delta t}$
Futures$1$

Discount at domestic $r$ in all cases. FX foreign rate acts as yield. Non-income forward $F_0=S_0e^{rT}$.

Under $Q$: $E^Q[S_{\rm next}\mid S]=Sa$. Physical stock probability $p^*=(e^{\mu\Delta t}-d)/(u-d)$. $p$ prices; $p^*$ forecasts. Non-dividend: $dS/S=\mu dt+\sigma dW$ becomes $r dt+\sigma dW^Q$ under $Q$; same volatility.

9 · CRR and multi-step tree

$N$ steps; $i$ time index; $j$ up moves.
$\Delta t=T/N,\ u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}},\ d=1/u$
$S_{i,j}=S_0u^jd^{i-j}$.

CRR matches local diffusion variance as step shrinks. Exact relative variance $p(1-p)(u-d)^2=a(u+d)-ud-a^2$. Convergence need not be monotonic.

Two-step European:
$f_0=e^{-2r\Delta t}[p^2f_{uu}$
$\qquad+2p(1-p)f_{ud}+(1-p)^2f_{dd}]$.

Terminal values: calls $\max(S_{N,j}-K,0)$; puts $\max(K-S_{N,j},0)$.

European: discounted continuation.
American: $f=\max(\text{continuation},\text{exercise})$ at every earlier node, including root. Recompute delta as stock/time changes.

10 · Backward induction pseudocode

set dt,u,d,a,p,disc=exp(-r*dt)
check d < a < u
for j=0..N:
  v[j]=payoff(S0*u^j*d^(N-j))
for i=N-1 down to 0:
  for j=0..i:  # ascending
    V=disc*((1-p)*v[j]+p*v[j+1])
    v[j]=V
    if American:
      v[j]=max(V,payoff(S0*u^j*d^(i-j)))
return v[0]

Time $O(N^2)$; rolling memory $O(N)$; storing whole tree $O(N^2)$.

11 · Stochastic notation & rules

L8: $z(t)$ level, $\Delta z$ increment, $\epsilon$ shock. L9 p.38: $u,v$ normal draws. Exercises: $W_t,X_t,U,V$; supplement: $Z(t)$. $N(m,v)$ = mean $m$, variance $v$. $\Phi$ = standard normal CDF.

$a(x,t)$: drift; $b(x,t)$: diffusion; $b^2$ = variance rate. This $a$ differs from binomial growth. $\mathcal F_t$: information at $t$; $E_t,\operatorname{Var}_t$: conditional moments.

Drift units: value/time; diffusion: value/$\sqrt{\rm time}$; variance rate: value²/time. Use years consistently (month $1/12$, quarter $1/4$).

$E[\sum Y_i]=\sum E[Y_i]$ always.
$\operatorname{Var}(\sum Y_i)=\sum\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)$
$\qquad+2\sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)$.

$\operatorname{Var}(aX+bY)=a^2\operatorname{Var}X$
$\quad+b^2\operatorname{Var}Y+2ab\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$.

Independent ⇒ uncorrelated (finite moments). Converse only with extra conditions, e.g. joint Gaussianity.

12 · Standard Wiener / Brownian

$z(0)=0$; continuous paths; independent increments on disjoint intervals; $z(t)-z(s)\sim N(0,t-s)$.

$E[z(T)]=0,\ \operatorname{Var}(z(T))=T$
$\operatorname{SD}(z(T))=\sqrt T$
$\Delta z=\sqrt{\Delta t}\epsilon$, fresh $\epsilon\sim N(0,1)$ each step.

Variances add; SDs do not. Quarter time → quarter variance, half SD. Continuous path ≠ differentiable path.

Markov: conditional future given present does not need earlier history. Does not imply independent levels. Time/state may be discrete or continuous.

Q · Standard $W_0=0$: scale, law, tail? For $\Delta t=.01$, $\Delta W=\sqrt{.01}\varepsilon=.1\varepsilon$. Interval $[2,5]$ has length $3$, so $W_5-W_2\sim N(0,3)$. Since $W_1\sim N(0,1)$, $P(W_1>1.5)=1-\Phi(1.5)=.0668$.

13 · Generalized Wiener: exact

Constant $a,b$: $dx=a\,dt+b\,dz$
$x(T)=x(0)+aT+b[z(T)-z(0)]$
$x(T)\sim N(x(0)+aT,b^2T)$
$\Delta x\sim N(a\Delta t,b^2\Delta t)$
$\operatorname{SD}(x(T))=|b|\sqrt T$.

Exact finite-step update:
$x(t+\Delta t)=x(t)+a\Delta t+b\sqrt{\Delta t}\epsilon$.

For $T>0,b\ne0$:
$P(x(T)<L)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{L-x(0)-aT}{|b|\sqrt T}\right)$.

Q12 · Cash reserve: law, moments, tail? $dX=8dt+12dW,X_0=30$; millions, years. Drift $8$, variance rate $12^2=144$. At 1 year: mean $30+8=38$, variance $144$ → $X_1\sim N(38,144)$. Half-year mean $30+8(.5)=34$; 2-year SD $12\sqrt{2}$. Below $25$ after 1 year: standardize $(25-38)/12=-13/12$ → probability $.1393$.

14 · Itô process: local moments

$dx=a(x,t)dt+b(x,t)dz$
$E_t[\Delta x]\approx a(x_t,t)\Delta t$
$\operatorname{Var}_t(\Delta x)\approx b^2(x_t,t)\Delta t$
$\operatorname{SD}_t(\Delta x)\approx|b(x_t,t)|\sqrt{\Delta t}$.

Freeze coefficients at current state. Exact for constant coefficients; generally approximate otherwise.

Euler: $x_{t+\Delta t}\approx x_t+a(x_t,t)\Delta t$
$\qquad+b(x_t,t)\sqrt{\Delta t}\varepsilon$.

Q14 · Monthly change and Euler next value? $dX=(4+.2X)dt+.15X\,dW$, current $X=80$, $\Delta t=1/12$. Substitute state first: $a=4+.2(80)=20$, $b=.15(80)=12$. Mean change $\approx20/12=1.6667$; variance $\approx12^2/12=12$. Next value $\approx80+20/12+\sqrt{12}\varepsilon$. The mean is a change, not the next level.

Choose the method

Constant $a,b$ → exact (§13). Variable $a,b$ → local/Euler (§14,17). Option price → $Q$ (§8–10). Real-world paths → $\mu$ (§16–17).

15 · Mean reversion

$dx_t=\kappa(\theta-x_t)dt+\eta dz_t$
$\kappa>0$: speed; $\theta$: target; $\eta$: diffusion. Drift positive below target, negative above. Variance rate $\eta^2$.

Q13 · Mean reversion: drift and local change? $\kappa=.6,\theta=50,\eta=8$. Drift $.6(50-X)$ at $X=40,50,65$ gives $6,0,-9$: toward 50, not a guaranteed path. At $40,\Delta t=.1$: mean change $\approx6(.1)=.6$; variance $\approx8^2(.1)=6.4$.

16 · GBM: dollars vs percentages

$dS_t=\mu S_tdt+\sigma S_tdz_t$
$dS_t/S_t=\mu dt+\sigma dz_t$.

Local changeMeanSD
Proportional$\mu\Delta t$$\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$
Dollars$\mu S\Delta t$$\sigma S\sqrt{\Delta t}$

Dollar variance $\sigma^2S^2\Delta t$. Drift coefficient $\mu S$; diffusion $\sigma S$; variance rate $(\sigma S)^2$. $\mu$ is annual expected proportional drift, $\sigma$ annualized volatility.

Local normal approximation ≠ exact long-horizon GBM return distribution. If $\sigma=0$: exact deterministic $S_t=S_0e^{\mu t}$.

Q15 · Monthly return vs dollar change? $S=120,\mu=.09,\sigma=.24$. Mean return $\approx.09/12=.0075$ (0.75%); SD $\approx.24/\sqrt{12}=.069282$ (6.9282%). Multiply each by $120$: dollar mean $.9$, SD $8.3138$; SD is not variance.

17 · Euler simulation

$S_{t_{k+1}}=S_{t_k}(1+\mu\Delta t+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\varepsilon_k)$

dt=T/N; S=S0
for i=0..N-1:
  draw fresh epsilon ~ N(0,1)
  S=S*(1+mu*dt+sigma*sqrt(dt)*epsilon)
  store S if a path is needed

Time $O(N)$ per path; $O(1)$ rolling state, $O(N)$ stored path. $M$ paths: $O(MN)$ time.

Use updated $S_i$, not always $S_0$. Independent shocks ≠ independent prices. Euler can give negative prices; smaller steps reduce discretization error, not randomness.

Q16 · Simulate 3 months? $S_0=100,\mu=.06,\sigma=.2$; shocks $.5,-1.2,.8$. First: $S_1=100[1+.06/12+.2(.5)/\sqrt{12}]=103.3868$. Reuse $S_1$ with shock $-1.2$ → $S_2=96.7408$; reuse $S_2$ with $.8$ → $S_3=101.6928$. Keep full precision; this is one path, not a forecast.

18 · Correlated increments

$\rho=$ correlation, $-1\le\rho\le1$.
$\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta z_1,\Delta z_2)=\rho\Delta t$
$\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta x_1,\Delta x_2)\approx\rho b_1b_2\Delta t$
$\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta S_1,\Delta S_2)\approx\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2S_1S_2\Delta t$.

Constant coefficients: exact increment covariance. Local correlation = $\rho$ for positive coefficients; exact for constant coefficients. Finite-horizon state-dependent changes need not have correlation $\rho$.

Q18 · Quarter-year joint changes? $dX_1=3dt+5dW_1$, $dX_2=2dt+7dW_2$, $\rho=.65$. Means $3(.25)=.75$, $2(.25)=.5$; variances $5^2(.25)=6.25$, $7^2(.25)=12.25$. Covariance $.65(5)(7)(.25)=5.6875$; divide by both SDs → correlation $.65$. Constant coefficients make these exact.

19 · Generate correlated normals

Independent $u,v\sim N(0,1)$:
$\varepsilon_1=u,\quad\varepsilon_2=\rho u+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}v$.

$E[\varepsilon_i]=0$; $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_2)=\rho^2+(1-\rho^2)=1$; $\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)=\rho$.

each step: draw fresh independent u,v
e1=u; e2=rho*u+sqrt(1-rho^2)*v
update each stock with its own e

Same $u$ within pair; fresh pair across time. $\rho=0$: independent; $\rho=\pm1$: perfect dependence.

20 · Brownian covariance identities

$\operatorname{Cov}(Z(s),Z(t))=\min(s,t)$
$\operatorname{Corr}(Z(s),Z(t))=\min(s,t)/\sqrt{st}$ for $s,t>0$.

Forward intervals $a\le b,c\le d$:
$\operatorname{Cov}(Z(b)-Z(a),Z(d)-Z(c))$
$=\max(0,\min(b,d)-\max(a,c))$
= overlap length.

General linear combinations:
$\operatorname{Cov}(\sum_i a_iZ(t_i),\sum_j b_jZ(s_j))$
$=\sum_{i,j}a_ib_j\min(t_i,s_j)$.

Linear Brownian combinations are jointly Gaussian: covariance $0\iff$ independence. For nonlinear functions, use actual independence properties; zero covariance alone is insufficient.

22 · Exam calculation patterns

Bounds: compute PV strike first. Parity: same $K,T$, check dividends and style. Profit: subtract premiums only after combining payoffs.

Tree: time units → $u,d,a,p$ → check $p$ → stock nodes → terminal payoff → backward discount → American exercise comparison → delta.

SDE: identify $a,b$ → substitute current state → variance rate $b^2$ → multiply by $\Delta t$ → SD square root. Mark exact/local.

Q17 · Quarter vs month: dollar moments? GBM $S=90,\mu=.07,\sigma=.25$. Use mean $\approx(.07)(90)\Delta t$, variance $\approx(.25\cdot90)^2\Delta t$. At $\Delta t=.25$: $1.575,126.5625$; at $1/12$: $.525,42.1875$. These are changes conditional on current price.

Q20 · Monthly joint dollar moments? $S_1=100,S_2=80$, $\mu_1=.08,\mu_2=.05$, $\sigma_1=.2,\sigma_2=.3$, $\rho=.5$. Apply $\mu_iS_i/12$ → means $.6667,.3333$; $\sigma_iS_i/\sqrt{12}$ → SDs $5.7735,6.9282$. Covariance $\approx.5(.2)(.3)(100)(80)/12=20$ (dollars²), not correlation $.5$.

23 · Common traps / scope

Variance is SD squared. Brownian level is not increment. $\Delta W$ scales with $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, drift with $\Delta t$. Uncorrelated need not mean independent. Risk-neutral probability is not physical probability. American value uses max, not just expectation.
